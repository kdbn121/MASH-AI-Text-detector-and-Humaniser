# Writing rules

These are the rules the lint enforces. They apply to every document produced for the
user and to every document audited by this skill.

## A. Content rules

### A1 Inflated significance

The text claims a subject is bigger than the subject. Signals: serves as a
testament to, stands as a testament, pivotal moment, a turning point in the
evolution of, marks a significant milestone, the enduring legacy of, underscored
the importance, highlights the significance, landmark achievement, transformative
potential, rapid evolution, rapidly evolving landscape, in today's fast-paced
world, has become increasingly important, plays a crucial role in shaping, a
reminder of.

Fix: state what the thing is and what it does. The framing is the claim, and the
claim has to survive without it.

### A2 Undue notability and media coverage

Signals: featured in, as seen in, widely covered by, garnered significant
attention, independent coverage, leading expert, active social media presence,
has been praised by, acclaimed, renowned, well known for.

Fix: name one source with a specific detail, or drop the claim.

### A3 Superficial analysis with -ing endings

The sentence opens a clause with a participle and then says nothing the clause
adds. Signals: highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting,
contributing to, cultivating, showcasing, driving, positioning, cementing,
solidifying, underscoring, fostering, enabling, empowering, unleashing,
paving the way, transforming the way.

Fix: replace with a concrete verb, or end the sentence and start a new one.

### A4 Promotional and advertisement-like register

Signals: vibrant, profound, groundbreaking, breathtaking, revolutionary,
must-visit, game-changing, stunning, nestled, seamless, transformative,
innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class, world-class,
unparalleled, meticulously crafted, meticulously designed, rich tapestry,
boasts, boasts a, it is not just X, it is Y.

Fix: plain, specific, neutral description.

### A5 Vague attributions and weasel words

Signals: experts say, experts argue, industry observers note, it is widely
believed, studies show, research suggests, many believe, some critics argue,
it is often said, according to many, it is generally accepted.

Fix: name the source and cite it, or drop the claim.

### A6 Outline-like challenges and prospects sections

A section that promises challenges and then promises a bright future, with no
specific fact in either half. Signals: despite these challenges, the future
looks bright, there is still work to be done, the potential is enormous, the
possibilities are endless, exciting times lie ahead, this is just the beginning,
only the beginning, the road ahead is long.

Fix: state the specific fact and the specific next step. If the section has
neither, cut the section.

## B. Language rules

### B1 Overused AI vocabulary

Delve, crucial, landscape, tapestry, foster, leverage, utilise, robust, dynamic,
testament, underscore, vibrant, pivotal, garner, interplay, realm, navigate the
complexities, in the realm of, myriad, plethora, seamless, elevate, empower,
unlock, harness, embark, journey, realm of, comprehensive, holistic, paramount,
crucial, vital, essential, profound.

Fix: the ordinary word. Look into rather than delve, use rather than leverage,
help rather than foster, show rather than showcase.

### B2 Copula avoidance

The subject is made to "serve", "stand", "mark", "represent", "boast", "feature"
or "offer" in place of a plain verb.

Signals: serves as, stands as, marks a, represents a, boasts a, features a,
offers a, provides a, hosts a, houses a, encompasses a, constitutes a.

Fix: use is, are, has, have, contains, holds.

### B3 Negative parallelisms

Signals: not just X but Y, not only X but also Y, rarely X without Y, more than
just, not merely X, but rather Y, it is not X, it is Y, this is not about X, it
is about Y.

Fix: state the point directly.

### B4 Rule of three abuse

Groups of exactly three descriptors or clauses appearing repeatedly. Signals:
innovation, inspiration and industry insights, speed, reliability and
affordability, clarity, precision and confidence.

Fix: keep three only when the content genuinely has three parts. Two or four is
fine.

### B5 Synonym cycling

The same object is renamed repeatedly for variety. Signals: the protagonist,
the main character, the central figure, the individual concerned, the subject
of the study, the party involved.

Fix: pick one term and use it.

### B6 False ranges

"From X to Y" where X and Y are not points on one scale. Signals: from startups
to enterprises, from students to professionals, from the classroom to the
boardroom, across the spectrum.

Fix: list the items.

### B7 Filler phrases

In order to, due to the fact that, at this point in time, it is important to note
that, it should be noted that, in the event that, for the purpose of, in the
context of, on the basis of, with regard to, with respect to, in terms of, the
fact that, needless to say, needless to mention, as a matter of fact, when all is
said and done, in today's world.

Fix: cut the phrase. Usually the sentence works without it.

### B8 Stacked hedging

Signals: could potentially possibly be, may perhaps, might conceivably, it could
be argued that, one might suggest, it seems that, arguably, to some extent,
somewhat, relatively, quite, rather.

Fix: pick a position. Hedge once, and only where the uncertainty is real.

## C. Style rules

### C1 Em dash and en dash

Neither character is used. The house rule is no em dash and no en dash. Use a
comma, a full stop, a semicolon, a colon or parentheses.

### C2 Excessive boldface

Bold applied to a phrase in nearly every paragraph, or to a phrase for emphasis
inside prose.

Fix: let word choice carry the emphasis. Use bold for structure only.

### C3 Inline header lists

Bolded label followed by a colon inside a paragraph: **Performance:** the speed
has improved, **Benefit:** reduces cost.

Fix: prose, or a real heading hierarchy.

### C4 Title case headings

Every word capitalised in a heading. Fix: sentence case.

### C5 Decorative emoji

Emoji used as bullet markers or section decoration.

Fix: remove unless the audience expects them.

### C6 Curly quotation marks

Curly quotes where the house style calls for straight quotes.

Fix: straight quotes in plain text.

### C7 Curly apostrophes

Curly apostrophes in prose. Fix: straight apostrophes in plain text.

## D. Communication rules

### D1 Chatbot artefacts

Signals: I hope this helps, I hope this is helpful, let me know if you have any
questions, let me know if, certainly, of course, absolutely, happy to help, feel
free to reach out, great question, you're absolutely right, I'm glad you asked,
thank you for reaching out, here's what I found.

Fix: remove every pleasantry. Answer the question.

### D2 Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers

Signals: as of my last update, as of my knowledge cutoff, up to my last training
update, based on available information, based on available data, I cannot access
real-time data, it is important to remember that my training data has a
cutoff date.

Fix: state the fact. Give the date if the date matters.

### D3 Sycophantic tone

Signals: great question, wonderful, fantastic, excellent point, you're absolutely
right, I completely agree, what a great insight, this is an excellent question.

Fix: answer without validating.

### D4 Generic conclusions

Signals: the future looks bright, exciting times lie ahead, this is just the
beginning, the possibilities are endless, only the beginning, the future is
bright, there is a long way to go, the journey has just begun.

Fix: end on a specific fact or a specific next step.

## E. Structure rules

### E1 Sentence length variation

A coefficient of variation below 0.35 across the body sentences indicates a flat
rhythm, which is one of the things the classifiers read. Aim for short sentences
next to longer ones. A run of sentences of near identical length is a finding
even when every word is plain.

### E2 Discourse marker repetition

The same opening connector repeated across the document: moreover, furthermore,
additionally, however, therefore, consequently, nevertheless, in addition.
Repeated use of the same marker at the head of sentence after sentence is a
finding.

### E3 Forced lists of three

Three-item lists in the body, counted across the document. A high count is a
finding. Keep the ones that carry real content, cut the rest.

## F. Citation and sourcing rules

- Cite only sources that exist. Verify the DOI, the URL and the author list.
- Never invent a statistic. If a number appears, the source is checkable.
- Never invent a quotation.
- Never rewrite a direct quotation. Quoted material stays quoted and stays
  attributed.
- A reference list entry the text does not cite, and a citation with no entry,
  are both findings.

## G. Fixed house rules

- British English spelling.
- No em dash, no en dash.
- Straight quotes and straight apostrophes in plain text.
- Sentence case headings.
- Body only for similarity and pattern scoring: the cover page (title page) and the
  reference list are excluded from the count.
